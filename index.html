Are NYC Rats Really in Retreat Under Eric Adams?

An analysis of 311 rat-sighting complaints from 2022 to 2025 shows steep declines in some neighborhoods — and uneven progress across the city.

When Mayor Eric Adams declared a “war on rats,” his administration pointed to falling 311 complaints as evidence of success. In a recent interview on Ziwe, Adams said, “I did a great favor for you and other New Yorkers. I took one hazard out of your life. The rats.” But an analysis of NYC 311 rat-sighting complaints from 2022 through 2025 shows a more uneven picture. Researchers caution that changes in 311 rat-sighting complaints do not always reflect actual changes in rat populations. Reporting behavior can shift depending on whether residents believe complaints lead to meaningful intervention. According to Michael Parsons, a senior investigator at the Centre for Urban Ecological Solutions, 311 data relies on residents taking the time to file a report — meaning fewer complaints don’t necessarily mean fewer rats. “A few things that could explain the patterns (without reflecting changes in the number of rats) include the dramatic shift in garbage set out times,” Parsons said. “The rats are still there… they are just more likely to come out when the garbage comes out.” He added that the removal of outdoor garbage sheds may also reduce reported sightings: “When people eat—outside—they see and they report. The rats are still there, but aren’t as easily spotted.” Parsons noted that post-pandemic work patterns, including remote work and irregular commuting hours, may also influence when — and whether — residents encounter rats. “All of this is to say that it is premature to say ‘the war is won,’” he said.
